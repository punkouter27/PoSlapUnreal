Sound

Engine. Web/audio.js has two engines behind the same cues. Synth (default on desktop above the low tier): every impact is synthesised when it happens. A material pair's resonator bank (modes: frequency, T60, amplitude) and contact noise are excited by a half-sine force pulse whose length follows Hertz contact (τ ~ v-1/5: a harder hit is shorter, so each mode, weighted by the pulse's spectrum, comes out brighter) and whose amplitude follows the collision energy ½ m v² (strike: the card's strike mass x palm speed; the rest from the contact probe's approach speed and effective mass). Flesh pairs start sharp and glide down (a soft contact stiffens as it compresses). Sliding soles drive a continuous friction voice per athlete: band noise whose level follows the shear speed x √(normal force) and whose centre rises with the speed, plus an intermittent stick-slip squeak on the deck. Spatial: every source is a PannerNode with panningModel HRTF at its place in the arena, the listener is the camera (impacts at the contact point, friction at the sliding sole, breath and grunts at each head). Geometric reverb: image sources off the floor (mat 0.35 / deck 0.8 by where the reflection lands), the podium's two faces (only if the path really meets the face), the bowl's four walls (0.3, people absorb) and the rig at 12 m, each delayed by its extra path, low-passed and HRTF/equal-power placed; ArenaIR.wav is the late tail. The crowd loop, its grains, the PA and the bell are unchanged. Samples (phones, the low tier, or View → Audio: samples; ?audio=samples|synth) is the 11-03 engine. Note: MuJoCo's mj_contactForce binding returns zeros into a JS array, so the probe decodes efc_force itself (pyramid edges summed).
